Major Requirements

The scientific study of the brain requires the skills of many different disciplines. Thus, this major is interdisciplinary and interdepartmental. The faculty are biologists, psychologists, biochemists, mathematicians, and engineers, all of whom share a fascination with the function of the brain. The brain is studied at many different levels, including the molecular and cellular levels, the level of systems of neurons, and the behavioral level.

On April 7, 2009, the Undergraduate Council of the Academic Senate, Curriculum Committee certified the Neuroscience, B.S. as a 
capstone major
. The Neuroscience capstone is a project-based culmination to the Neuroscience curriculum. In the Neuroscience major, the capstone requirement is satisfied by either participating in independent research under the guidance of a faculty mentor, through enrolling in a research laboratory course and an extra elective, or participating in neuroscience outreach via DOPA-Team or Project Brainstorm. Completion of one of these capstone options provides students with an in-depth exposure to neuroscience research and provides a culminating capstone experience for Neuroscience majors.

Important Notes — Preparation for the Major

All major preparation courses must be taken for a letter grade, and each course must be passed with a C- or better.

All major preparation courses must be completed with an overall GPA of 2.0 or better.

All honors or enhanced versions of any courses listed above are also acceptable for completion.

Students receiving grades below C- in more than two major preparation courses, either in separate courses or repetitions of the same course, are subject to dismissal from the major.

Important Notes —

Independent Research

Students must have completed NEUROSC M101A and an SRP 99/199 in the same research lab with the same faculty mentor (who holds an Academic Senate title) prior to enrolling in Neuroscience 198AB/199AB.

Students must be involved in a research lab at UCLA (non-UCLA research labs not permitted), and their research projects must be Neuroscience-related in order to enroll in Neuroscience 198AB or 199AB.

The Neuroscience 198AB and 199AB research contract courses must be taken in consecutive quarters; Neuroscience 198AB must be taken within the same academic year.

NEUROSC 198/199 research contracts are 4 units each. Letter grades are assigned upon completion of NEUROSC 198B/199B, earning the student 8 total units. Grades for NEUROSC 199A/198A will remain as “IP” (in-progress) indefinitely, but the 4 units still count towards study list units. 

Maximum of 8 units of courses 198A, 198B, 199A, 199B may be applied toward major.

Students interested in earning additional upper division units for their research involvement may enroll in NEUROSC 199C or SRP 199 under their faculty mentor’s home department.

Neuroscience Poster Day
 held during the Spring quarter.

Important Notes — Project Brainstorm

Project Brainstorm is a two-quarter outreach and research project offered to juniors and seniors in the Neuroscience major, in which students have an opportunity to develop teaching lessons on Neuroscience that are presented to local K-12 students.

Project Brainstorm meets on Thursdays from 9:00 am – 11:50 am in both the Winter and Spring quarters. However, there will be additional requirements and an increased time commitment to satisfy the capstone.

Students interested in Project Brainstorm must apply during the Fall when applications become available.

Students must have completed or be concurrently enrolled in NEUROSC M101A at the time of application.

Submission of an application does not guarantee acceptance.

Participation is required at the annual 
Neuroscience Outreach Day
 held during the Spring quarter.

Important Notes — DOPA-Team

DOPA-Team is a two-quarter outreach and research project offered to juniors and seniors in the Neuroscience major, in which students have the opportunity to choose a novel, unique project, such as youth drug trends over time during the winter quarter, and be given the opportunity to choose a drug category and translate the academic knowledge acquired in C177 into an age-appropriate and interactive presentation and hands-on activity for high school students during the spring quarter.

Students interested in DOPA Team must apply during the Fall when applications become available.

Students must have completed or be concurrently enrolled in NEUROSC M101A at the time of application.

Submission of an application does not guarantee acceptance.

NEUROSC C177 may not be applied toward the major elective requirements for DOPA Team students.

Participation is required at the annual 
Neuroscience Outreach Day
 held during the Spring quarter.

Important Notes — Upper Division Major Requirements

All upper division major courses must be taken for a letter grade, and students must have an overall GPA of 2.0 or better in the upper division major requirements.

Students must earn a C- or better in NEUROSC M101A to advance in the series.

No more than 8 upper division courses may be taken under the Neuroscience department for the major. Students should aim to complete at least one major elective within another department to avoid exceeding this limit.

A maximum of 8 units of NEUROSC 198 or 199 (in any combination) may be applied toward the major.
