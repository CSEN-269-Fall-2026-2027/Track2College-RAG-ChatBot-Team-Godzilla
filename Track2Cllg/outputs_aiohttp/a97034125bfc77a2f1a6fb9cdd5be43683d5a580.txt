Political Science at Westmont: think deeply and critically about political life
The Department of Political Science invites students to study politics in the broader context of the Christian liberal arts. At Westmont, students of political science acquire core competencies in the theory and practice of politics across the discipline, including the subfields of political theory, international relations, and American politics. Throughout the major, students are challenged to think, read, and write broadly and well on topics such as democracy, justice, and war. Beyond this, we seek to cultivate citizens and scholars whose understanding and affections reflect Christian theology and practice, who are attuned to interconnections with other disciplines, and who have a posture to work faithfully for justice and the common good in a world characterized by political, social, cultural, religious, and economic pluralism.

Experiential Learning Opportunities

Internships:

All Political Science majors complete an internship course, with opportunities at the local, state, and federal levels and across public and private sectors. Students have interned with non-profits, law offices, think tanks, members of Congress—even the White House.
Class Simulations:

Upper-division courses on Congress, Constitutional Law, and International Organizations and Law include significant simulation components. You might play the part of a congressional party whip trying to reform health care; or issue a decision on religious freedom as the chief justice of the U.S. Supreme Court in a mock trial; or you might travel to New York City as part of our award-winning Model United Nations delegation.
Off-Campus Programs:

Political science majors expand their global education through a range of off-campus opportunities, including semester-long programs in Mexico, Europe, and beyond, plus a variety of Mayterm offerings.

Westmont at the NMUN in New York City 2026

Eighteen students from the POL 112: 
International Organizations and Law
 course traveled to New York City to represent Slovakia at the National Model United Nations conference, joining nearly 3,000 peers from around the world. Throughout the week, students engaged complex global issues—from nuclear weapons to sustainable transportation, demonstrating thoughtful collaboration, diplomacy, and the strength of their liberal arts education. 
Their preparation and professionalism stood out, with several delegates receiving recognition from peers for their character and effectiveness in committee sessions. Four students, Chaz Keith, Samson Rakhshani, Emily Lindblad, and Rahel Kebede earned awards for outstanding position papers, highlighting their academic excellence. The trip also included a visit to the United Nations General Assembly Hall and time exploring New York City, making for a memorable and impactful learning experience.
