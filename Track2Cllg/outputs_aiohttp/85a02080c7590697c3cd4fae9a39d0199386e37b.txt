UG-SLO 1: Writing Effectiveness

The program evaluated undergraduate writing proficiency using two direct measures embedded within Writing Intensive Courses (WIC): 
SMR 330 
and
 SMR 221

During this assessment cycle, undergraduate students comfortably exceeded the target benchmarks across both operational levels:

Measure 1 (SMR 330):
 Out of 33 students assessed using the 
Undergraduate Writing Skills Grading Rubric
, 30 students reached or exceeded the proficiency threshold. This represents a 
91% success rate
, thoroughly exceeding the program's stated performance expectations.

Measure 2 (SMR 221):
 Out of 40 students assessed via the 
Undergraduate Research Paper Grading Rubric
, 34 students achieved proficiency. This represents an 
85% success rate
, also exceeding the established threshold.

The data indicate strong overall performance, demonstrating that the current curriculum effectively supports student development in professional and academic writing. The higher success rate in the upper-level course (
SMR 330
) compared to the lower-level course (
SMR 221
) suggests positive scaffolding and maturation of student writing skills as they progress through the sport management and recreation curriculum.

Action Plan for Continuous Improvement- 
While the quantitative targets were exceeded, the program remains committed to a continuous cycle of improvement. To maintain this momentum and further elevate student outcomes, the sport management faculty will implement the following action steps:

Rubric Calibration- 
To ensure the integrity and consistency of the assessment data across different sections and instructors, faculty teaching 
SMR 221
 and 
SMR 330
 will conduct a rubric calibration session. To verify that student writing aligns with evolving industry demands, faculty will audit the current 
Undergraduate Writing Skills Grading Rubric
. This review will ensure emphasis is placed on modern professional communication formats, including executive summaries, data-driven briefing memos, and strategic industry proposals.

Formative Scaffolding in Lower-Level Courses- 
While the 85% success rate in 
SMR 221
 is commendable, it leaves room to support the remaining 15% who did not meet proficiency. Faculty will introduce smaller, formative writing benchmarks earlier in the semester (such as draft-and-revise cycles for outlines and literature syntheses) to catch struggling writers before the final research paper submission.

Integration of Campus Writing Resources- 
Faculty will actively collaborate with the university writing center to build targeted workshops or integrated tutor support specifically for 
SMR 221
. Directing students to these institutional resources early in their major coursework will establish stronger baseline technical skills.

Performance Status: Exceeds Expectations

The operational goal was significantly exceeded, with an aggregate mean score of 4.9/5.0, tracking 0.4 points above the institutional target of 4.5. This exceptional performance across all three faculty members (Gilbert, Kay, and Sharpe) points to high instructional quality, strong student engagement, and consistent course delivery. Individual scores were tightly clustered between 4.84 and 4.90, indicating that teaching effectiveness is uniform across sections rather than concentrated in a single high performer.

Contributors to this success include targeted mid-semester check-ins, proactive adjustments based on early student feedback, and robust alignment between course objectives and assessments.

Action Plan for Continuous Improvement:
 While the current benchmarks indicate exemplary performance, maintaining this trajectory requires deliberate, continuous operational support. To "close the loop" and leverage these findings for future improvement, the department will implement the following actions:

Pedagogical Modeling:
 Faculty members with outstanding evaluation trends (4.8+) will be invited to share best practices, syllabus designs, and student communication strategies during upcoming departmental workshops.

Target Calibration:
 Because the aggregate score (4.9) sits near the ceiling of the current 5.0 scale, the department will evaluate whether the target threshold of 4.5 should be raised to 4.6 for the next assessment cycle to encourage continued incremental growth.

Qualitative Review:
 In the next cycle, quantitative evaluation scores will be cross-referenced with qualitative student comments to identify specific behavioral drivers (e.g., responsiveness, clarity of grading rubrics) behind these high metrics.
