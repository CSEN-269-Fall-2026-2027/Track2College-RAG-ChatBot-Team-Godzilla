                     Get Your Hands Dirty with TROY's Anthropology Degree 

                     Looking for a degree that combines history, sociology and science? The anthropology
                     major at Troy University prepares you to enter a fascinating and diverse field with
                     rewarding career opportunities. Our program encompasses all four subfields of anthropology:
                     Archaeology, Biological Anthropology (including Forensic Sciences), Cultural Anthropology
                     and Linguistics. TROY's unique focus on archaeology and forensic anthropology, and
                     it's extensive hands-on lab and field experience, help make the TROY program one of
                     the best in the U.S.

                  “TROY's program provides students with an opportunity to study and experience a multidisciplinary
                  approach, a necessity for anyone pursuing a career centered on anthropology. Such
                  an emphasis highlights the importance of practical versatility for excelling both
                  academically
                  and professionally in a field that aims to understand what it means to be human. The
                  foundation of my career is built on the dedication and diligence that TROY not only
                  provided to me, but to all of its students.”

John Robert "Robby" Elmore
 | Collections Curator, Alabama Department of Archives and History | 2017 graduate,
                  Bachelor of Science in Anthropology

                     Work with Artifacts and Participate in Digs as You Earn Your Degree in Anthropology

Choose a program that no other university in Alabama can rival for its depth of field
                        experience. You'll learn research methods from experienced archaeologists and anthropologists
                        in your courses. Then, you'll put those methods into practice through internships,
                        independent research and study abroad programs specifically geared toward anthropology
                        students. Along the way, you'll have the opportunity to work in modern anthropology
                        labs. You'll also be able to participate in excavations and archaeological projects
                        throughout the southeastern U.S. and Italy. You can even work part time as an undergraduate
                        at the TROY Archaeological Research Center (TUARC).

                     Preparation for Graduate Studies in Anthropology, Archaeology and Related Fields 

If you're interested in deeper research projects or teaching at the university level,
                        a BS in anthropology from TROY prepares you for graduate work in various fields, including
                        applied anthropology, archaeology, medical anthropology, museum studies and forensic
                        anthropology. Our graduates have gone on to advanced degree programs at the University
                        of Alabama, the University of Tennessee, the State University of New York (SUNY) system,
                        the University of Virginia and others. You can also study law and anthropology through
                        TROY's accelerated six-year 
BS/JD program for anthropology majors
.

Suggested Minors for the Anthropology Degree 

TROY offers two optional interdisciplinary minors that anthropology majors can choose
                  between to further their career goals and explore their interests. 

Archaeology Minor

Archaeology is a subset of anthropology that focuses on studying human history and
                        culture via physical remains. With this minor, you'll build upon the archaeology coursework
                        required for the major and gain more knowledge of and hands-on experience with different
                        methods and field research practices. The minor is interdisciplinary, allowing you
                        the flexibility to take courses from other departments, so you can specialize in areas
                        related to anthropology, like history, geography or chemistry.
Catalog - Archaeology Minor

Forensic Science Minor

If you're drawn to forensic anthropology, TROY is the place to gain hands-on experience.
                        For example, in 2022, forensic science students 
assisted the Pike County Sheriff's Department
 in the search for the remains of a 2012 murder victim. In addition to learning about
                        forensic techniques, ethics and evidence, the forensic science minor will give you
                        a more extensive biology, anatomy and psychology education than the anthropology major
                        alone, but with a focus on courses that apply to forensic sciences not found in any
                        of TROY's science bachelor's programs.

FAQ About the Anthropology Degree 

Learn more about the field of anthropology and our program.

What is anthropology?

Anthropology is a field that studies humanity and its culture, taking into account
                        history, language, religion, social structures, economics, politics, biology and the
                        environments in which people live. Anthropology includes four subfields:

Archaeology,
 which focuses on studying humanity via the discovery and analysis of material remains,
                           including artifacts, structures and settlements.

Biology, 
which focuses on human biology and that of their ancestors, including evolution, genetics
                           and variation across time and location.

Social/Cultural, 
which focuses on human societies and their relationships with one another.

Linguistics, 
which focuses on language, how it relates to humanity and its cultures and how it
                           evolves over time.

Studying anthropology can prepare you for careers within the field, along with jobs
                        in many others, such as criminal justice, museum studies, archaeology and historic
                        preservation, public health, education, international development and law. At TROY,
                        you'll join students from all walks of life who come together to study and understand
                        culture and cultural change.

What is forensic anthropology?

Forensic anthropology is the study of human remains in the context of legal/criminal
                        investigations. It combines the subfields of biological anthropology and archaeology.
                        Forensic anthropology uses empirical evidence, genetic information and computer technology
                        to determine the physical characteristics of human specimens. If you want to help
                        solve crimes involving human remains, studying forensic anthropology at TROY can prepare
                        you for exciting and rewarding careers.

What can you do with an anthropology degree?

A bachelor's in anthropology can lead to career opportunities in numerous fields and
                        prepare you for graduate study. Studying anthropology provides you with skills nearly
                        every industry values, including critical thinking, research, communication and cultural
                        awareness. Specific fields you can work in include education, public health, government,
                        research, environmentalism and criminal justice.

Jobs within the field of anthropology include:

Anthropologist

Archaeologist

Cultural resource manager

Epidemiologist

Forensic scientist

Historians

Museum curators

Sociologist

Do I need a master's degree to work in anthropology?

No, working in anthropology doesn't require a master's degree. You can pursue anthropology-related
                        jobs with a bachelor's in anthropology, such as contract archaeology or cultural resource
                        management (CRM) jobs. But, like other professions, a higher degree leads to even
                        more opportunities.

After earning your BS in anthropology, you'll be well-positioned to follow TROY graduates
                        into advanced degree programs at institutions such as the University of Alabama, the
                        University of Tennessee, the State University of New York (SUNY) system and the University
                        of Virginia. Master's and PhD degrees can qualify you to work in advanced research
                        roles, universities, public policy, cultural resource management, healthcare, nonprofit
                        leadership and other specialized fields that require deep expertise in human behavior
                        and culture.

What is the average salary for anthropology jobs? 

According to the 
U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS)
, in May 2024, the median annual wage for anthropologists and archeologists was $64,910.
