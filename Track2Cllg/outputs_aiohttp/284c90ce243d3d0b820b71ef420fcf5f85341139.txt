							Philosophy & Theology at The King's University						

Wrestle with what it means to "be." Examine and critique ideas as you pursue God, truth, goodness, and beauty. Dig into different forms of reasoning while exploring major developments in the history of Western thought and theology.

Inside This Program

Journey Through The History Of Human Thought

Philosophy and theology have developed alongside human civilization and great intellectuals have always been central to radical social changes. Unpack works from pivotal scholars and thought-leaders from throughout the centuries who devoted their lives to a pursuit of truth.

Discover Philosophy and Theology's Relevance Today

Together, philosophy and theology teach us what it means to be made in the image of God. Thoughtfully examine our human nature in relation to contemporary topics like political engagement, war and peace, gender and sexuality, and environmental stewardship.

Grow In Faith

Reflect on what scripture and the gospels means for your own life and for all of humanity. By comparing Christianity to other religions and worldviews, you'll discover its most distinct and compelling characteristics.

Think For Yourself

Challenge your assumptions with good questions as you debate perspectives and theories with professors and classmates. We can tell you all about philosophy and theology, but our goal is to set you up for a lifetime of building your own convictions.

Spiritual Formation for Civic Life in the Neo-Calvinist Tradition

•

																	Dr. Michael DeMoor															

•

Faculty of Arts and Social Science

This paper offers an overview of how the neo-Calvinist tradition (originating in the late nineteenth century Netherlands) understood and practiced spiritual formation in order to prepare its members for civic life. It reflects on how the distinctive understanding of public life in the tradition shaped these practices and suggests that this helps to explain the contemporary situation of the movement.

Christian Nationalism and the Project of Christian Political Theory: A Review Essay of The Case for Christian Nationalism by Stephen Wolfe

•

																	Dr. Michael DeMoor															

•

Faculty of Arts and Social Science

Stephen Wolfe’s The Case for Christian Nationalism presents “Christian Nationalism” as a comprehensive Christian political theory. The article analyzes his claims regarding national will and agency and find them to be deeply problematic. These problems arise from his methodological assumptions about Christian political theory. In place of those assumptions, the article recommends Kuyper’s idea of architectonic critique as a more fruitful approach to Christian political theory in the Reformed tradition.

Faith Negotiating Loyalties: An Exploration of South African Christianity through a Reading of the Theology of H. Richard Niebuhr

•

																	Stephen W. Martin															

•

Faculty of Arts and Social Science

Faith Negotiating Loyalties draws readers into the world of Christian faith in South Africa and the question of loyalties in the new post-apartheid state. It carries out its investigation in two parts. Part one examines Christian faith and loyalty during the first nation-building exercise following the South African War, positioning the creation and contestation of three Christianities corresponding to three nationalisms, each of which imagined South Africa in a particular way, shaping faith accordingly.
