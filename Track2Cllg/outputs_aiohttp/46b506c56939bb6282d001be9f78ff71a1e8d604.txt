Why earn your Political Science degree at St. Edward’s?
Whether you're interested in public service, law, diplomacy, public policy or political advocacy, you'll graduate ready to make a meaningful impact wherever your career takes you.
 Study Politics Where It Happens
Austin is an ideal location for the study of politics. As the capital of Texas, the city gives you direct access to the state legislature, state agencies, nonprofit organizations, advocacy groups and elected officials. Internships and legislative experiences put you at the center of the policymaking process.
 Develop a Global Perspective
At St. Edward's, you'll study political systems outside the United States and address challenges that cross borders. Study abroad programs put you on the ground in other countries, while faculty with international expertise connect classroom work to real-world global dynamics. You'll graduate with a perspective that reaches well beyond Austin.
 Learn from Faculty Who Challenge and Inspire
Small class sizes mean you'll build meaningful relationships with professors who are accomplished scholars, mentors and advisors. They'll help you refine your interests, connect classroom learning to real-world experiences and prepare for graduate school, law school or your first career after graduation.
 Build Your Network for What's Next
Every Political Science major completes at least one internship, giving you valuable professional experience before graduation. Students have interned with congressional offices, the Texas Legislature, nonprofit advocacy organizations, political campaigns and public policy groups, building connections that open doors to careers across the country.

Life as a Political Science Major at St. Edward's University
One day you might be attending committee hearings at the Texas Capitol. The next, you'll debate constitutional issues in class, interview public officials for The Civics Lab podcast or intern with a nonprofit working to influence public policy. Along the way, you'll collaborate with classmates who share your passion for leadership and social change while learning from faculty who encourage thoughtful discussion and diverse perspectives.
You'll graduate not only understanding how political systems work—but also how to help improve them.

Frequently Asked Questions about a Political Science degree from St. Edward's University

What will I learn as a Political Science major at St. Edward's University?

What You Will Learn
How to critically analyze political institutions and processes at the local, national, and international levels using social science theories and evidence-based reasoning
How to research, assess, and synthesize information to solve real-world political and policy problems
How to communicate complex political and social issues clearly and persuasively in writing and speech, for professional and public audiences
How to evaluate political questions through multiple ethical and cultural perspectives, with an understanding of the responsibilities that come with civic and professional life
Scholarships, Conferences and Summer Institutes
Political Science majors have been selected for many competitive learning opportunities domestically and abroad. They include:
The Benjamin A. Gilman International Scholarship, sponsored by the State Department, which offers grants to undergraduates to study or intern abroad in locations including Chile, Brazil and Morocco.
The State Department Critical Language Scholarship, an intensive overseas language and cultural immersion designed to expand the number of Americans mastering foreign languages that are critical to national security and economic prosperity.
The Public Policy and International Affairs Junior Summer Institute at Carnegie Mellon University, a highly competitive seven-week program that trains students in the quantitative, analytical and communication skills they’ll need to study public policy in graduate school and enter a career that serves the public good.
The highly selective Harvard Kennedy School Public Policy Leadership Conference, which introduces students to graduate programs in public policy at Harvard.
The John Jay Fellows Program in Philadelphia, a semester-long leadership development program based on Christian principles that prepares participants for careers in public service.

What are the requirements for Political Science degree at St. Edward's?

Major Requirements:
 The Bachelor of Arts in Political Science requires 42 hours of major courses. Students complete 21 hours of core courses and 21 hours of political science electives from the following areas of study: American Politics, Political and Civic Engagement, International Politics and Pre-Law.
General Education Requirements:
 The degree requires 45  hours of general education courses that students complete over four years in addition to their major courses and electives.
Certificates
: Students will have the opportunity to complete two certificates in Political Science.  The “Certificate on Law, Policy and Politics” allows students to explore the relationship between the legal and political realms. The “Certificate on International Security and Geopolitical Risk Analysis” allows students to explore transnational issues of interest to governments and employers.

 Making an Impact
Political Science alumnus and Journalism minor Jacob Sanchez ’16 
combined his interest in civic life with a passion for telling stories that matter. At St. Edward’s, he developed his skills inside and outside the classroom, leading the student newspaper Hilltop Views and completing internships with The Texas Observer and The Texas Tribune.
After graduation, Jacob began his career covering local news before joining the nonprofit newsroom Fort Worth Report as an education reporter. His in-depth reporting on issues affecting schools and communities has earned recognition from the Texas Managing Editors.
By pairing political science with journalism, Jacob built a foundation for understanding the institutions and policies that shape people’s lives — and for asking the questions that help communities better understand them. Today, he continues to pursue the work he discovered he loved at St. Edward’s: reporting stories that shine a light on K–12 education.
