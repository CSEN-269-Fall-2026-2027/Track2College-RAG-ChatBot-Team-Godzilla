A versatile, hands-on program to compose, design, and engineer.

TAC students at SFCM cultivate creativity at the intersection of music and technology. Our students immerse themselves in the latest music technology, from spatial audio and Ambisonics to AAA video game and film scoring, but do so from a place of grounded, classical compositional study.
The TAC program is a fast track to becoming a one-stop production studio, with courses in audio engineering and mixing, sound design, and emerging media like augmented reality. Three academic focuses—Film Scoring, Game Scoring, and Composer-Performer—allow students to dial in their path at SFCM, and the Conservatory's world-class Studio G is our TAC playground, where students can bring whatever they hear into being.
Department faculty are active composers, working on shows like Netflix's One Piece and video games Death Stranding 2. SFCM's ties to Bay Area leaders like Sony, Dolby, SFFILM, and Skywalker Sound provide endless opportunities for students to learn from their future collaborators and employers, whether that's scoring SFFILM entrants or writing and recording a spec score for a fictional video game—with real-time, onsite feedback from members of Sony's audio team—as part of our twice-yearly Sony Project.
SFCM's ties to the San Francisco Symphony benefit TAC students as well: In 2025, iconic composer Danny Elfman stopped in for a Q&A with the department and took students backstage at his two-night run at the Symphony. Three-time GRAMMY winner Ricky Kej, Michael Abels (Jordan Peele's go-to composer), and 
Musical America's
 2025 Composer of the Year Gabriela Lena Frank are just a few of TAC's visiting faculty as well.

TAC's new studios in the Bowes Center, inaugurated in late 2021, are home to digital Avid S6 and analog API 1608-II consoles, as well as a Meyer Sound 9.1.6 Dolby Atmos monitoring system, making them the perfect place for audio recording, post-production, and spatial audio experimentation.

Award-winning film composer Michael Abels met one-on-one with TAC students during a campus visit.

Each semester visiting artists from Sony Interactive Entertainment work with SFCM’s TAC students to record student scores for a fictional video game.

Students work with industry professionals in Studio G, the TAC program's technology hall, to adjust the variable acoustic and truss system in preparation for recording.

50 Oak Street's Studio A, a space designed for large-scale and advanced recording projects as well as clinics and master classes with visiting artists and professionals.

Bay Area's NPR affiliate KQED said, "One could call TAC the perfect marriage of technology, classical musicianship, and experimentation."

Each January, SFCM students participate in Winter Term, a dedicated period of exploration outside one's primary focus area. During Winter Term, TAC students have participated in projects such as building hardware synthesizers, projection mapping in Hume Concert Hall, and performing with modular synths in a public concert.

TAC plays host to SFCM's "Future is Female" concert in honor of International Women's Day.
