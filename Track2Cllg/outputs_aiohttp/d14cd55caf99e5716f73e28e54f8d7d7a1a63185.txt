Theology

Faith Seeking Understanding

At the University of Dallas, theology is the study of the Mystery of God and of His
                        marvelous works, especially Jesus Christ. The journey takes root in close readings
                        of the Sacred Scriptures and involves discipleship of paths trod by great doctors
                        of the Catholic Tradition, such as Thomas Aquinas, Augustine of Hippo, Gregory Nazianzus,
                        John Henry Newman, et al. We also study the works of recent masters, such as Pope
                        John Paul II, Henri de Lubac, Garrigou-Lagrange, Pope Benedict XVI, et al.

Our desire to contemplate divine truth leads us also to grapple with difficult questions
                        and to engage the best critics of the Catholic faith. Thus, we aim to achieve mature
                        appreciation of Catholic truth as the deepest and most resilient answer to man’s hunger
                        to know who he is and why he exists. We foster habits of careful reading, mastery
                        of essential principles, perusal of ideas, reasoned argument, and written and oral
                        expression. Such habits serve our alumni above all in the pursuit of sacred wisdom
                        but also in professional and practical life.

Why Study Theology at UDallas?

Catholic orthodoxy

Rest assured that the deposit of faith, contained in both Sacred Scripture and Sacred
                              Tradition and clarified by the Magisterium, is the foundation of all our inquiries
                              and the measure of all our claims.

Intellectually Rigorous

Relish the challenge of puzzling questions and high-level objections to our faith.
                              Deepen your confidence that Jesus Christ founded his Church to remain for all time.
                              Draw on robust philosophical (and other) thinkers in pursuit of intellectual excellence.

Four ‘Habitus’ of Theology

Explore the beauty of Sacred Scriptures, and trace the winding paths of history. Strive
                              for universal expressions in systematic theology, and study beatific happiness and
                              human life as ordered thereto in moral theology.

Faith, Reason, & Dialogue

Enjoy the dialogues implicit in the Catholic “and”. Faith and reason. Revelation and
                              science. Ancient thought and new insight. Catholic orthodoxy and ecumenical explorations.

Great Texts and Masters

Focus on the classic texts themselves, whether ancient or recent, and the great masters
                              of our tradition. Use research and secondary literature in aid of the former task.

Professional Preparation

Learn to understand others’ ideas and to sift them in a fair and balanced light. Research,
                              reason, write, and present. These skills prepare any graduate for a wide range of
                              professional occupations.
