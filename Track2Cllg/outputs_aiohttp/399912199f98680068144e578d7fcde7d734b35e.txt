3 Reasons to Study French

1.

French is the second most studied language in the world. It is spoken on all five continents, in the halls of international diplomacy, and in the homes of over 320 million people.

2. 

Discussions of the biggest challenges facing humanity are conducted in French: climate change and ecological conservation, poverty and economic development, settler colonialism and self-determination, independence and oppression, revolution and political unrest.

3.

A valuable career asset, proficiency in French is a significant advantage everywhere French is spoken. With your degree in French, you might develop new financial platforms for West Africa, help preserve Caribbean wildlife, practice international law, or work with immigrants navigating a new life.

Ici, tout le monde parle français 
In our language courses, everyone speaks French. French 101, 102, and 201 are the language sequence, which gives you mastery of the basics. You’ll be able to hold conversations, share your opinions, fight a parking ticket, watch movies, get a job, rent an apartment, and reflect on the future of global food shortages, all in French.  

Immersion is a way of life  
Learn the language in three semesters, live the culture for the rest of your life. The French program at Austin College gives you the tools to actually use your French: living in the language house, cooking and baking with native speakers, trips abroad via JanTerm or semester-long opportunities. You’ll find yourself dreaming in French. 

What kind of career are you interested in?  
Our courses foster your passion for changing the world. If you’re fascinated by economics, you’ll learn about the history of capitalism and political revolutions. If you’re bookish, you’ll love the depth of reading literature in the original French. If you’re keen to find innovative solutions to ecological disaster, you'll look towards solutions proposed in French-speaking Africa and the Caribbean. Our courses are rooted in social justice—seeing the world for how it could be—and positive change.
