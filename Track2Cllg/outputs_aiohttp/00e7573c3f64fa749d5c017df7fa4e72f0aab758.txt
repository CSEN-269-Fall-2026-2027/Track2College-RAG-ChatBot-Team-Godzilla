Why Earn Your Graphic Design Degree at St. Edward’s?
Graphic design is about more than creating beautiful visuals—it's about communicating ideas, solving problems and making meaningful connections. At St. Edward's, you'll build your creative and technical skills through collaborative studio experiences, faculty mentorship and hands-on projects that prepare you to design with purpose in today's creative industries.
 Learn from Dedicated Faculty
Work alongside experienced designers and educators who are invested in your growth as both a creative professional and an individual. Small studio classes and close faculty mentorship provide personalized feedback, meaningful collaboration and the support to help you refine your creative voice.
 Explore an Innovative Curriculum
Develop expertise across typography, branding, motion graphics, publishing, design history and social impact while exploring how visual communication shapes the way people interact with the world. During your senior thesis, you'll define your own creative direction and build a portfolio that reflects your interests and career goals.
 Create in Professional Studios
Bring your ideas to life using interdisciplinary creative spaces, including Mac labs, photography studios, publishing and production labs, animation facilities and specialized printmaking and fabrication equipment. You'll gain hands-on experience with the tools and technologies used throughout today's creative industries.
 Build Real-World Experience
Apply your design skills beyond the classroom by contributing to campus publications like

Life as a Graphic Design Student at St. Edward's University
Great design doesn't happen in isolation. As a Graphic Design major, you'll join a collaborative studio community where creativity, feedback and experimentation are part of the design process. Through Command G, the program's student organization, studio visits, workshops and connections with Austin's creative community, you'll build relationships with classmates, faculty, alumni and industry professionals. Along the way, you'll build a professional portfolio, gain real-world experience through internships and graduate with the confidence to pursue a creative career.

Frequently Asked Questions about a Graphic Design Degree from St. Edward's University

What will I learn as a Graphic Design major at St. Edward's University?

Students in the Graphic Design program will focus on developing their own voice as a graphic designer, defining your own trajectory in design.
A few examples of courses students take in the major:
In Interactive Design, explore design issues unique to user-centered interaction in digital media. Examine a range of formal and conceptual issues, including user interface, organization, narrative, motion, time and sound.
In Social Design, explores issues of responsibility and how designers participate, shape and criticize the world around us.
What skills will you gain?
Discover your style by studying image making, typography and experimentation.
Understand the evolution of art by focusing on design history and social impact.
Define your own practice as designers and focus on an area of personal interest—everything from branding to digital product design and entrepreneurship.

What are the requirements for a Graphic Design degree at St. Edward's?

The BA in Graphic Design requires 49 hours of graphic design major courses, which include a combination of introductory coursework, visual studies, art, and graphic design.
General Education Requirements: The Graphic Design degree requires 57 hours of general education courses that students complete over four years in addition to their major courses and electives.

 Making an Impact
When the 2024 total solar eclipse passed over Central Texas, Associate Professor of Graphic Design Jimmy Luu turned the historic event into a creative challenge for his students. In his Risograph Workshop, students designed eclipse-inspired calendars, magazines, glasses, packaging and other original pieces while gaining hands-on experience with specialized risograph printing.
Their work traveled beyond the classroom to Riso Rama, an independent publishing event in Corpus Christi, where students exhibited and successfully auctioned their designs, with proceeds benefiting the St. Edward’s Graphic Design student organization. One student’s “Eclipse Gum” packaging even caught the attention of Miau Editions, an independent press based in Mexico City.
The project gave students the opportunity to experiment with a distinctive design process, respond creatively to a cultural moment and share their work with a broader creative community.
